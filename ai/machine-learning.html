Machine Learning: Learning From Examples

Introduction

Machine learning is the part of AI that actually does the "learning." The basic idea is simple: show a computer program a large pile of examples, let it guess an answer, tell it whether the guess was right or wrong, and let it slightly adjust itself to do better next time. Repeat that process millions of times, and the program gradually gets very good at the task, even though nobody ever explicitly told it the rules. It is closer to how you got better at free throws through practice than to how you memorized a times table.

There are a few main flavors of this. In supervised learning, every example comes with the correct answer attached, like a photo labeled "cat" or "not cat," and the program learns by comparing its guesses against those known answers. In unsupervised learning, there are no labels at all, and the program just looks for patterns or groupings on its own, like noticing that certain customers tend to buy similar products. In reinforcement learning, the program learns through trial and error by getting rewards or penalties, similar to training a dog with treats.

What "training" a model actually means ▶

Inside a machine learning model are a huge number of adjustable numbers, often called weights, that control how it turns an input into an output. At the start, these numbers are basically random, so the model's guesses are terrible. Training means feeding it examples, measuring how wrong its guess was using something called a loss function, and then nudging every one of those numbers slightly in the direction that would have made the guess a bit less wrong. This nudging process, repeated over an enormous number of examples, is what slowly turns a random guesser into a genuinely useful model.

Why more data usually means a better model ▶

If you only ever saw pictures of golden retrievers, you might wrongly assume all dogs are golden and fluffy. A machine learning model has the exact same problem, called overfitting to a narrow slice of examples. Feeding it a much larger and more varied set of examples, different dog breeds, angles, lighting, and backgrounds, forces it to learn the actual general pattern of "dog" rather than memorizing specific pictures. This is a big part of why modern AI models are trained on massive datasets: variety and volume genuinely help the model generalize to situations it has never encountered before.
