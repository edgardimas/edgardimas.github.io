Intro: What Even Is AI

Introduction

Think about how you learned to recognize a dog. Nobody handed you a rulebook that said "four legs, fur, a tail, barks." You just saw enough dogs, in enough shapes and colors, that your brain figured out the pattern on its own. Artificial intelligence, or AI, is software built around that same idea: instead of a programmer writing exact rules for every situation, the software learns patterns from huge amounts of examples, then uses those patterns to handle new situations it has never seen before.

This is a big shift from older software. A calculator app follows a fixed rule: 2 plus 2 always equals 4, written directly into the code by a human. An AI system that recognizes photos of cats was never given a rule for "catness." Instead, it was shown millions of labeled photos and slowly adjusted itself until it got good at telling cats from everything else. AI is not one single technology, it is really a whole family of techniques, and the family member getting the most attention today is called machine learning.

AI vs a normal computer program ▶

A normal program is basically a giant list of instructions: if this happens, do that. Every possible situation has to be thought through and written by a human ahead of time, which works great for things like a calculator or a to-do list app. AI flips this around. Rather than writing the rules directly, engineers write a system that can discover its own rules by studying data. This matters because some tasks, like recognizing a face or understanding a sentence, have so many possible variations that no human could ever write rules to cover them all by hand.

Narrow AI vs the sci-fi kind of AI ▶

Every AI system in use today, from spam filters to voice assistants to chatbots, is what is called narrow AI, meaning it is good at one specific type of task and nothing else. A chess-playing AI cannot suddenly decide to write you a poem unless it was separately built and trained to do that too. The general, do-anything AI you see in movies, often called artificial general intelligence, does not exist yet. It is a serious research goal, not something running quietly in your phone right now, no matter how impressive today's tools can seem.
